Media Gateway · merged and running

Every frame and every sample your service processes arrives through here.

The Media Gateway takes the live audio and video coming off the glasses and hands it to Vision and Speech as ordinary Python objects — images as arrays, audio as PCM. This page covers what it does, why it's built this way, and how to build your service on top of it.

Want to see it work first? Path A is one command and puts your own camera through the pipeline. Writing code straight away instead? Path B needs nothing running at all.

01 — What it does

One component between the glasses and everything else

Smart glasses stream first-person audio and video to a workstation. That stream is continuous, high-rate, and lossy — it drops out when someone walks past the microwave, and it reconnects on its own.

The gateway sits directly on that stream and does five things:

  • Subscribes to the live audio and video — the only component in the system that does.
  • Decodes compressed video into raw pixels and audio into raw samples.
  • Samples video down to 2 frames per second, because running detection on 30 fps is waste, not accuracy.
  • Filters frames that aren't the size they should be — real streams emit malformed 8×8 frames during reconnects, and one of those reaching a detector is a silent correctness bug.
  • Relays the result to Vision and Speech over a plain WebSocket, and carries synthesized speech back to the wearer.
02 — Why it exists

The alternative was three copies of the same hard problem

Vision and Speech could each have subscribed to the glasses directly. That's the obvious design, and it's worse in four specific ways.

If everyone subscribedWith one gateway
Every service decodes the same video separately — the most expensive thing in the pipeline, done twice. Decoded once. The same immutable bytes are handed to every consumer.
Every service implements reconnect handling, jitter buffering, and frame sampling. Three implementations, three sets of bugs, and they disagree under load. One implementation, tested once. Everyone gets identical behaviour.
Every service holds credentials for the media server, widening what an attacker gets from any one of them. One component holds credentials. Consumers hold none.
Swapping the transport later means rewriting three services. Consumers depend on a WebSocket and Python objects. The transport underneath can change without touching them.

The boundary is drawn deliberately: all live-media concerns stay above the gateway — codecs, network negotiation, jitter, reconnects, audio return. Below it, nothing knows those exist.

Where this came from

This isn't speculative design. A dedicated experiment ran first, three full cycles with deliberate disconnects, and it produced the sampling policy, the frame filter, and the reconnect rule you'll read about below. The findings are in the spike results.

03 — What you get

The interface, and what it guarantees

Two WebSocket endpoints, one per media kind. Connect and iterate.

EndpointYou receive
/v1/stream/videoJPEG frames at 2 fps, decoded for you as frame.rgb — a height × width × 3 numpy array
/v1/stream/audioRaw PCM, 48 kHz mono, in 100 ms chunks as chunk.pcm
/v1/return-audio/{id}Send PCM in and it plays out of the wearer's earpiece
/v1/statusEverything the gateway currently believes, as JSON

Guarantees you can build on

  • Video never blocks the stream. If your detector is slow, you get the newest frame and older ones are discarded — the gateway tells you how many, so you can measure your own gaps. A slow consumer degrades itself and nothing else.
  • Audio is never dropped. Silently losing audio corrupts a transcript invisibly. If a consumer falls too far behind, its connection is closed with an explicit reason instead. Loud failure over quiet corruption.
  • Control messages are ordered with media. You can never receive a frame before the message announcing the stretch of video it belongs to. That ordering is what makes the reset rule below implementable at all.
  • Reconnects are handled for you. Drop your connection and the client reconnects with backoff, then re-announces anything still in progress so you resynchronise correctly.

Message types you'll see, in the order they typically arrive:

MessageMeaning
stream_helloFirst on every connection. Tells you what's already in flight.
session_startedA device connected.
epoch_startedReset your per-track state now. See the rules.
video_frame · audio_chunkThe media itself.
epoch_endedThat stretch of video ended, with a reason.
session_endedThe device went away.
keepaliveSent while idle, so silence is distinguishable from a dead socket.

Full specification: Media Relay Contract.

04 — Terms

Vocabulary used in the code and the other docs

WebRTC
The technology behind a video call — live audio and video with minimal delay. Built for “right now”: it degrades quality rather than falling behind. It's what the glasses speak.
LiveKit
The server that receives those streams and routes them to whoever should get them. We run our own copy locally; nothing goes to a cloud service.
Track
One stream from one source. The camera is one track, the microphone another. They travel independently — which is why video and audio are handled separately throughout.
Session
One continuous period of a device being connected, identified as sess_01JAB…. Video and audio from the same wearer share it, so it's how you correlate them.
Epoch
An uninterrupted stretch of video from one track. It ends whenever the connection drops, even briefly. The concept most likely to cost you a day — see the rules.
Frame rate
We relay 2 frames per second, not 30. Enough to see what happened, cheap enough to run real models on every one of them.
Path A

See it working with your own camera

One command · needs Docker · start here

The fastest way to understand what this service does is to watch your own face go through it. This brings up the media server and the gateway together, and prints a URL that turns your laptop into a stand-in for the glasses.

everything, in one command 
cd services/media-gateway && ./scripts/dev_up.sh

It generates credentials on first run and reuses them afterwards, starts the media server, verifies what that server is listening on, launches the gateway, and waits until it is genuinely ready. Ctrl-C stops everything it started.

That is the gateway alone. To put a real camera through it you also need the console, so ./scripts/dev_stack.sh from the repository root is usually what you want — it starts LiveKit, all four services and the console together.

Open http://localhost:5173 and press Publish. On WSL2, open it in your Windows browser; that is where the camera physically is.

in a second terminal — watch your camera arrive as frames 
cd services/media-gateway && uv run python -m visual_memory_media_contract.tap ws://127.0.0.1:8080/v1/stream/video

Three things to try, watching that output as you go:

  • Speak — a tone plays from your speakers. That is the return path the assistant will eventually talk through, working end to end with no Speech service present.
  • Disconnect, then Rejoin — the camera track SID changes while the session and identity stay put. That is a Wi-Fi blip, reproduced on demand, and it is the case the rule below exists for.
  • Inspect what the gateway believes:
the gateway's own view of the world 
curl -fsS localhost:8080/v1/status | python3 -m json.tool

Look at guard.dimensions first — a tally of every frame size that arrived, counted before anything is filtered. It separates “nothing is connected” from “something is connected but every frame is being rejected,” which are otherwise indistinguishable from outside.

Useful flags
  • --scripted — skip the media server entirely and run on synthetic video. That is Path C in one command.
  • --port 9000 — if 8080 is taken.
  • --keep-livekit — leave the server up when you stop the gateway, so restarting is fast.
What the script is doing, if you would rather run the steps yourself

Credentials must match between the media server and the gateway. A mismatch fails in a way that looks like a network problem rather than a configuration one, which is the main reason this is a script and not three lines in a README.

the long way 
export VMA_LIVEKIT_API_KEY=vma-dev
export VMA_LIVEKIT_API_SECRET=$(openssl rand -hex 24)

docker compose -f compose.dev.yaml up -d livekit
python3 tools/dev-livekit/check_listeners.py

cd services/media-gateway
VMA_MEDIA_SOURCE=livekit VMA_DIMENSION_GUARD_MODE=first_frame_wins \
  uv run uvicorn media_gateway.main:app --port 8080

The listener check is not ceremony. During the spike the media ports turned out to be listening on every network interface while everything appeared correctly bound to localhost.

first_frame_wins matters too: the frame filter defaults to expecting 320×180 and rejecting everything else, and your webcam is probably 1280×720 — so every frame would be correctly discarded and nothing would arrive.

Path B

Build your service against recorded sessions

Nothing running · where Vision and Speech will spend their time

Real sessions were recorded and shipped as files, so you can develop and test your whole service with no gateway, no media server, no camera, and no network. This is the loop you want on a plane.

once 
cd packages/media-contract && uv sync --frozen --all-groups && uv run pytest
in your own tests 
from visual_memory_media_contract import MediaClient
from visual_memory_media_contract.testing import assert_matches_fixture, replay_server

async with replay_server("video_session_basic") as url:
    received = [message async for message in MediaClient(url, reconnect=False)]

assert_matches_fixture(received, "video_session_basic")

Both recordings contain deliberate trouble, because that is what makes them worth testing against:

  • video_session_basic — a session that drops and reconnects partway through, plus a dropped frame and a clean shutdown. Handle this file correctly and you handle a real Wi-Fi hiccup.
  • audio_session_basic — three seconds of audio with a deliberate half-second hole. Code that counts messages will never see it. Code that reads the sample counter will. That is the entire purpose of the file.

Also available: flaky_replay_server, which severs the first connection mid-stream to exercise recovery.

Your tests and the gateway's tests assert against the same files. If a change to the gateway would break your service, both sides go red — that is the point.

Path C

Run the gateway on synthetic video

One command · no camera, no media server, no Docker

Between the other two: the real service and the real network path, driven by video the gateway generates itself. Useful when you want to test against a live socket but do not want to be on camera.

terminal 1 — the gateway 
cd services/media-gateway && ./scripts/dev_up.sh --scripted
The mistake everyone makes once

Run uv from inside services/media-gateway. From the repository root it resolves a different environment and fails with ModuleNotFoundError: No module named 'media_gateway'.

terminal 2 — watch the messages 
cd services/media-gateway && uv run python -m visual_memory_media_contract.tap ws://127.0.0.1:8080/v1/stream/video
expected output 
stream_hello   active_epochs=[{'epoch_id': 'TR_VCscripted30', ...}]
epoch_started  epoch_id='TR_VCscripted30' identity='scripted-glasses' 320x180
video_frame    seq=0 320x180 jpeg 8586B sha=f64ac9482b3f dropped=4
epoch_ended    epoch_id='TR_VCscripted30' reason='track_unsubscribed'
epoch_started  epoch_id='TR_VCscripted31' identity='scripted-glasses' 320x180
video_frame    seq=0 320x180 jpeg 8586B sha=f64ac9482b3f dropped=4

Three things in that output matter:

  • epoch_id changed while identity stayed the same — a reconnect. Same wearer, new stretch of video.
  • seq restarted at 0. Counters do not carry across a reconnect.
  • dropped=4 is the sampler shedding frames to keep up. Expected — you are seeing 2 of every 30.
05 — Create your service

Scaffolding, and the one dependency line that is easy to get wrong

Once you can consume the stream you need somewhere to put that code. Services are generated, not hand-written — the generator produces the layout, Dockerfile, config, health endpoints, and lockfile that the repository validator requires.

from the repository root 
python3 .agents/skills/visual-memory-repo-standards/scripts/new_service.py vision \
  --kind worker --owner "Person 1"

--kind is application, worker, or inference. Vision and Speech are workers. Use inference only if you need platform-specific model packages — it carries extra deployment obligations.

Depending on the media contract

Your service consumes the relay through packages/media-contract. It is a local package, not something on PyPI, so it needs a source mapping. Add both pieces to your pyproject.toml:

pyproject.toml 
[project]
dependencies = [
    # ...whatever the generator gave you...
    "visual-memory-media-contract",
]

[tool.uv.sources]
visual-memory-media-contract = { path = "../../packages/media-contract", editable = true }
editable = true is load-bearing

Leave it out and uv copies the package into your environment. uv sync --frozen then reports everything as satisfied, so nothing looks wrong — but your service keeps running a stale snapshot of the contract, and every fix to the shared package silently passes you by. It presents as “the gateway is sending me the wrong thing.”

This cost real debugging time on the gateway itself. It is one word, and it is the difference between a package that tracks the repository and a copy that quietly rots.

then, from your service directory 
uv lock && uv sync --frozen --all-groups

Verify it resolved the way you want — this should print a path inside packages/media-contract, not one inside .venv/lib:

confirm the wiring 
uv run python -c "import visual_memory_media_contract as m; print(m.__file__)"

One consequence worth knowing: the lockfile records the local package's resolved dependencies, not its source. If someone adds a dependency to media-contract, every consuming service needs to re-run uv lock.

python3 .agents/skills/visual-memory-repo-standards/scripts/validate_repo.py
!! — Required of every consumer

The rule that will cost you a day if you skip it

When you receive epoch_started, discard everything you were tracking.

the shape of every consumer 
async for message in MediaClient("ws://localhost:8080/v1/stream/video"):
    match message:
        case EpochStarted():
            tracker.reset(message.epoch_id)      # ← this line
        case VideoFrame():
            tracker.step(message.rgb, message.captured_at)
        case EpochEnded():
            tracker.finalize(message.reason)

Why

Object trackers assign numbers: object 42 is the keys. Those numbers only mean anything within one continuous stretch of video.

The wearer walks past the microwave. Wi-Fi stutters for two seconds. The connection re-establishes on its own and the person never notices — but your tracker started counting from scratch, so object 42 is now the cat.

Nothing about this resembles an error. Both readings are confident, both internally consistent. The system ends up certain the keys are somewhere they have never been, and no confidence score anywhere is low, because nothing failed. The gateway can detect the discontinuity and tell you; it cannot reset your tracker for you.

Four consequences
  • A reconnect on your side counts too. The client re-announces anything still running, so handling that one message covers both cases.
  • Video and audio carry different epoch ids — separate tracks. Correlate them with session_id.
  • For audio, trust pts_samples rather than how many messages arrived. It's the only way a gap becomes visible.
  • Ignore header fields you don't recognise. New optional fields will be added and must not break you.
06 — Status

What's verified, and what isn't

AreaState
Video and audio relay, sampling, frame filteringverified
Reconnect and epoch handling, across real disconnect cyclesverified
Return audio to the wearerverified
Real camera and microphone through a browserverified
Deployment topology — health, auth, isolationverified
Signals telling Memory a track was lostneeds sign-off
A device publishing from another machine over a real networkneeds hardware
Open decision — Memory

The gateway can report that a track disappeared, but it has no idea which objects were being tracked, so it can't name them. Who fans that out to individual objects, and on which endpoint, needs a decision. Three specific questions are written up in the Data Contract. Until that's settled these signals appear on the relay only and nothing is sent to Memory — so don't build against that shape yet.

House rules

Four things that will fail review

  • Secrets in the repository. Not in code, logs, images, or compose files.
  • Anything leaving the machine. No cloud speech, hosted model APIs, or tunnels on the default path. A test watches for connections to non-local addresses and fails on any — this is the promise the product makes to the person wearing the glasses.
  • Logging raw media, transcripts, or tokens. Log a size and a digest instead.
  • Adding requirements.txt, Poetry, or a second Python version. Every service owns a uv.lock, and Python is 3.11 — a dependency dropped 3.11 in its newest release, so the lockfiles hold it back deliberately.
